# Accessibility Fellows
Since 2024, Research Software Asia Australia has been running the Accessibility Fellowship Program

Every year, people can apply to be an Accessibility Fellow. 

The RSAA26 Accessibility Fellowship aims to encourage individuals with disabilities - such as those who are deaf, hard of hearing, blind, have low vision, or have other disabilities - from within our community or adjacent communities to join RSAA26. Their involvement will help ensure we are held accountable for the accessibility of this online event.

To strengthen gender diversity in our accessibility fellowship, we especially encourage women and gender fluid disabled people to apply. We value the unique perspectives and experiences you bring.

Successful fellows will be paid an initial sum to engage with the Equersa organising committee in a way that is comfortable for them and for them to provide feedback on how Equersa could be more accessible and inclusive - before, during and after the conferences. After the event the fellows will provide us with a report that Equersa can share with the community.

## Suggestions for successful fellows

We also want to remind successful fellows that:
1. Please don't overwhelm yourself with taking on too much, so please be gentle to yourselves.
2. Please make sure you center yourselves. We are doing this to help humanise you and why accessibility helps you be included - so if you look at the previous accessibility reports, they all tried to be centered around their needs and why it was important to them - not WCAG standards.
3. Please think about how you can use this to promote yourself and the advocacy work that you do - this is just as much about benefiting you as helping us
4. Please think about how we can make it easier to help others who may have struggled with the fellowship application and
5. Please remember we are a community conference run by volunteers - we may not be able to implement your suggestions - in fact we are struggling to keep up with our commitments to do the Accessibility Fellowship and the micro-grants.
